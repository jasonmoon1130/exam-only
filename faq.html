Common questions

Can I take an AP exam without taking the class?

Yes. You sign up as an exam-only student through a school that orders AP exams. You can't register with the College Board on your own, so you need a school's AP coordinator to add you. The guide walks through it.

How much does it cost?

The College Board fee is $99 per exam in the U.S. for 2026–27. Some host schools add their own fee, and you usually pay the host school directly.

If cost is a problem, ask your counselor about fee reductions, or take the class free through AP Access for ALL, which also covers the exam fee.

When do I need to ask?

As soon as you can. Schools order exams by November 13, 2026, and many want your payment before that. Schools can still order late, until March 12, 2027, but late orders cost extra.

What if two of my exams are at the same time?

You can still take both. Tell the AP coordinator when you register, and they'll arrange late testing for one of them.

What if every school says no?

Search by ZIP code to see schools farther away, try private and religious schools, and ask your district office who hosts AP testing. You can also call AP Services for Students at 888-225-5427. The guide has the full list.

Do colleges care that I self-studied?

Colleges see the same AP score whether you took the class or studied on your own. They don't penalize you for courses your school doesn't offer.

Does EXAM ONLY send emails for me?

No. It writes the email and opens it in your own email app, so you read it and send it yourself. Replies come straight to you.

Is my information saved anywhere?

Only on your own device. Your name, school and My schools list stay in your browser. We don't have a server, so we never see them.

How does EXAM ONLY keep me safe?

There are no accounts and no passwords. Everything you type stays in your own browser, and the site never sends it anywhere. Your emails go out from your own email account, so replies come straight to you.

On a shared computer, use "Clear my info from this device" on the My schools page when you're done. And before you send any email, check the address, and never email a password, your Social Security number or card numbers.

Where does the school list come from?

From the U.S. Department of Education's list of every public school, for the 2023–24 school year. Private schools aren't listed yet. Phone numbers can change, so check the school's website before you call.
